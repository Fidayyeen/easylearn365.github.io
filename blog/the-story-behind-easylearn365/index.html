The Story Behind EasyLearn365

Published on July 18, 2026 Website Build Journey 7 min read

Originally published on Bytes & Clouds and rewritten for EasyLearn365.

Introduction

Welcome to EasyLearn365.

This website is more than a personal website or technical blog. It is a place where I document my learning journey, projects, experiences, and lessons learned throughout my career in technology.

Over the years, I have worked across different IT roles, earned Microsoft certifications, and developed a passion for cloud technologies, identity management, automation, and modern workplace solutions. Along the way, I discovered that some of the most valuable learning happens when we build, document, and share what we learn with others.

EasyLearn365 was created with that purpose in mind.

In this post, I would like to share how my journey began, what shaped my career, and why I decided to build this platform.

How I Entered IT

Looking back, it is interesting how life often makes decisions for us before we fully understand where they might lead.

I did not come from a formal computer science or IT background, and my academic path was never specifically designed to lead me into technology. Yet technology had already become part of my life long before I entered the industry.

My earliest memory of computers goes back to 2005, when I first stepped into my school computer lab as a student in the 4th standard. Like many children of that era, I was fascinated by what seemed like a machine capable of doing almost anything.

What started as curiosity gradually became a long-term interest.

I spent hours exploring computers, experimenting with software, creating simple HTML pages using forms and marquees, and learning basic networking concepts. One of the books that introduced me to programming was the classic Let Us C. I was not following a formal curriculum, but it gave me an early appreciation for logical thinking and problem-solving.

I was also fascinated by networking, Wi-Fi technologies, network topologies, and the early internet era. I still remember the excitement of using dial-up internet and seeing how quickly technology was changing the way people communicated and accessed information.

Years later, I entered the professional world as a Technical Support Specialist. What seemed like an entry-level role became the perfect starting point. It exposed me to real-world systems and business environments while turning a lifelong interest in technology into a professional journey.

Learning Through MSP Experience

As my experience grew, I moved into Help Desk support within a Managed Service Provider (MSP) environment.

Unlike supporting a single organization, working in an MSP exposed me to multiple businesses, technologies, and operational environments. Every organization had its own priorities, challenges, and ways of working, which accelerated my learning significantly.

I gained practical experience across enterprise infrastructure, security, and day-to-day operations while solving real-world problems for customers.

One of the most valuable lessons I learned during this period had little to do with technology itself. For the first time, I began to understand the business side of IT.

I saw how organizations struggled with growth, collaboration, security, remote work, and operational efficiency. More importantly, I realized that technology was not the end goal. It was a tool for solving business problems.

This was where Microsoft's ecosystem began to stand out to me. I saw how Microsoft 365 and Azure solutions could work together to improve productivity, strengthen security, and support business growth through a connected platform rather than isolated tools.

The question was no longer just: "How does this technology work?" It became: "What business problem does it solve?"

Discovering Cloud Technologies

As I gained more experience, I found myself increasingly drawn toward Microsoft's ecosystem.

What initially caught my attention was Microsoft 365 in the cloud and Active Directory in on-premises environments. I was fascinated by how Microsoft's solutions worked together as part of a larger platform rather than as standalone technologies.

Driven by curiosity, I invested more time in hands-on learning. I built Windows and Linux server labs, experimented with enterprise technologies, and explored how different systems interacted with one another. These labs allowed me to learn by doing: breaking things, rebuilding them, and developing a deeper understanding of infrastructure and operations.

Around the same time, I began pursuing Microsoft certifications to structure my learning and expand my knowledge. Through labs, self-study, and certifications, I was gradually introduced to Azure and the broader cloud ecosystem.

Looking back, this was the point where my career began shifting from supporting technology to understanding and building the platforms that power modern businesses.

Why I Created EasyLearn365

As my interest in cloud technologies and modern workplace solutions grew, I realized I needed a structured way to continue learning and advancing my career.

Alongside certifications, I spent countless hours learning through Microsoft Learn, technical documentation, community resources, and hands-on exploration.

One lesson became clear early in my journey: certifications open doors, but hands-on experience builds confidence.

During this period, I also developed a habit that would later shape EasyLearn365: documentation. Whether learning a new technology, solving a problem, or exploring a new concept, documenting what I learned helped me understand, retain, and build upon that knowledge over time.

EasyLearn365 was created as a place to bring everything together. It serves as a personal website, technical blog, project portfolio, and public learning journal. Rather than showcasing only finished results, I want to document the process of learning, building, troubleshooting, and improving over time.

As my journey continues, my focus is expanding from modern workplace, identity, security, Azure, automation, and DevOps toward AI enablement and AI Platform Engineering.

For me, AI Platform Engineering is not a separate destination from the rest of my career. It builds on the same foundations: cloud infrastructure, identity, governance, security, automation, documentation, and practical problem-solving.

Key Takeaways

  • Curiosity can become the foundation for a long-term technology career.
  • Real-world support and MSP experience teach both technology and business context.
  • Microsoft 365, Azure, identity, and security make more sense when viewed as a connected platform.
  • Certifications help structure learning, but hands-on work builds confidence.
  • EasyLearn365 exists to document the journey: build, document, and improve.

Closing Thoughts

When I first walked into a school computer lab in 2005, I could never have imagined where that moment of curiosity would eventually lead.

Every role, project, challenge, certification, and learning experience has contributed to the person and professional I am today.

EasyLearn365 is my way of documenting that journey and sharing what I learn along the way. Whether you are just starting in IT, exploring cloud technologies, or building your own learning path, I hope something here helps you learn, build, and grow.
